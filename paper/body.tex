\abstract
% context
{Astronomical archives answer positional queries from relational databases, and in the
PostgreSQL world two extensions dominate: Q3C, which keys each source by an integer
drawn from a quad-tree on a cube and stores it in a B-tree, and pgSphere, which stores
spherical geometry in a GiST tree. The first is compact and fast for points; the second
is general and indexes regions as well.}
% aims
{We ask whether the two can be combined: a single index that keeps the integer key and
the B-tree, yet answers cones, polygons, cross-matches and stored footprints in
archives' own geometry language, and whether it can beat each established extension on
its own ground.}
% methods
{We present \texttt{skycell}, a PostgreSQL extension that keys positions by their
order-29 HEALPix cell and covers a query region with cells. How finely to cut is decided
by a cost model -- the price of an extra index range against the false-positive rows it
removes -- with local source density read from the histogram \texttt{ANALYZE} already
keeps. Cones and cross-matches are answered by a planner custom scan that walks the
covering's ranges; polygons by a rewrite into range conditions; stored regions by a
GiST opclass family. Coverings are validated by brute force over millions of points.
Performance is compared with Q3C 2.0.5 and pgSphere 1.5.2 on PostgreSQL 18.6, on 10-
and 50-million-row catalogues -- synthetic, resampled from the Gaia DR3 density field,
and real DR3 positions -- in randomized paired trials, warm and cold.}
% results
{Warm, \texttt{skycell} beats pgSphere at every cone radius from 1\arcsec\ to 3\degr\ on
all three corpora, by 17--33\% on the synthetic two and 4--31\% on real positions; cold
it is faster or level at every radius, and up to 2.7 times faster at 50 million rows. It
cross-matches 20--33\% faster than \texttt{q3c\_join} and about three times faster than
pgSphere, and answers convex polygons 2.7--4.4 times faster than pgSphere. Its index is
a third the size of pgSphere's and builds 11--23 times faster: 38\,GiB against
160\,GiB at Gaia DR3 scale for the pgSphere-plus-Q3C pair covering the same queries. Its
one cost parameter is derived from the relation's own statistics, and a \texttt{float4}
box key makes its region GiST faster than pgSphere's native GiST on every operator.}
% conclusions
{A cost-based covering over an equal-area integer key, answered by its own scan node,
beats each established PostgreSQL sky index at its own speciality while speaking ADQL's
geometry vocabulary and holding circles and polygons in one column. That makes it a
strong choice for an archive's positional layer.}

\section*{Data availability}

The extension, its test suites, the benchmark harness and the raw results of every
measurement quoted here are available at
\url{https://github.com/jesusjuansalgado/skycell} under the MIT licence, together with
the full history of the measurements that led to the current defaults. All corpora but
the real one are generated from seeds; the real one is drawn from the Gaia archive by a
recorded query.
